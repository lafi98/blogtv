A crochet pumpkin is one of the most satisfying autumn projects you can make. There are no rounds to count and no increases: you crochet a small ribbed rectangle in half double crochet (UK half treble crochet), seam it into a tube, gather the ends, and pinch it into segments with a few wraps of yarn. Add a stem and a leaf, and it's ready for a windowsill, mantel, or harvest table.

This pattern suits beginners who can chain and work half double crochet, and it's quick enough to make a whole patch in different sizes and colors. For a refresher, see our guide to [the basic crochet stitches](/blog/basic-crochet-stitches).

## Pattern at a glance

- **Skill level:** Beginner
- **Finished size (approximate):** about 4 in (10 cm) wide and 3 in (7.5 cm) tall, not counting the stem. Size varies with your yarn, tension, and stuffing.
- **Yarn weight:** Medium / worsted (#4). The Craft Yarn Council's [yarn weight system](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards/yarn-weight-system) explains the numbered categories.
- **Hook:** 5 mm (US H-8)
- **Gauge note:** Exact gauge isn't critical, but the fabric should be firm enough that stuffing doesn't peek through. If your stitches look gappy, try a 4.5 mm (US 7) hook.

## What you'll need

- Worsted-weight yarn in a pumpkin color: approximately 50–80 yd (45–75 m), an estimate that depends on your tension
- Small amounts of brown or green worsted yarn for the stem, leaf, and tendril: roughly 5–10 yd (5–9 m) in total
- 5 mm (US H-8) crochet hook
- Tapestry needle
- A small handful of polyester fiberfill or other stuffing
- Scissors
- Optional: a stitch marker and a few pins

## Abbreviations (US terms)

- **ch:** chain
- **sl st:** slip stitch
- **sc:** single crochet (UK double crochet)
- **hdc:** half double crochet (UK half treble crochet)
- **dc:** double crochet (UK treble crochet)
- **BLO:** back loop only
- **st(s):** stitch(es)
- **rep:** repeat
- **[ ]:** the stitch count at the end of a row or round

## Pattern notes

- The body is a flat rectangle worked in rows, then seamed into a tube.
- The ch 2 at the start of each body row does not count as a stitch. Work your first hdc into the very first stitch and your last hdc into the last stitch, so every row has 20 hdc.
- The foundation chain sets the pumpkin's height; the number of rows sets how far it reaches around.
- The odd number of rows is deliberate: it leaves one yarn tail at each open end of the tube, ready for gathering.

### Working in the back loop only

From Row 2 onward, every hdc goes into the back loop only. The top of each stitch forms a sideways "V" of two loops: the front loop is closer to you, and the back loop is farther away. Insert your hook under the back loop alone. The unworked front loops form the raised ridges that become your pumpkin's ribs.

## The pattern

### Pumpkin body

With your pumpkin color and leaving an 18 in (45 cm) tail, ch 22.

**Row 1:** Hdc in 3rd ch from hook (the 2 skipped ch do not count as a st) and in each ch across. [20 hdc]

**Row 2:** Ch 2 (does not count as a st), turn. Hdc BLO in each st across. [20 hdc]

**Rows 3–31:** Rep Row 2. [20 hdc in each row]

Don't fasten off. The rectangle should measure roughly 5½–6 in (14–15 cm) wide (the length of a row) and 11–12 in (28–30 cm) from Row 1 to Row 31, about twice as long as it is wide. If it's noticeably shorter, add rows in pairs so the total stays odd.

### Stem

With brown or green yarn and leaving a 6 in (15 cm) tail, ch 6.

**Row 1:** Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. [5 sc]

**Row 2:** Ch 1 (does not count as a st), turn. Sc in each st across. [5 sc]

**Rows 3–6:** Rep Row 2. [5 sc in each row]

Fasten off, leaving a 12 in (30 cm) tail.

### Leaf (optional)

With green yarn, ch 8.

**Round 1:** Sc in 2nd ch from hook, hdc in next ch, dc in next 3 ch, hdc in next ch, sc in last ch. Rotate the piece to work along the other side of the foundation chain. Sc in the free loop of the same ch as the last sc, hdc in next ch, dc in next 3 ch, hdc in next ch, sc in next ch. Sl st in the skipped ch at the tip. [14 sts, not counting the sl st]

Fasten off, leaving an 8 in (20 cm) tail.

### Tendril (optional)

With green yarn, ch 15.

**Row 1:** 3 sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. [42 sc]

The crowded stitches twist the strip into a corkscrew. Fasten off, leaving an 8 in (20 cm) tail.

## Finishing

### Seam the tube

With the working yarn still attached after Row 31, fold the rectangle so the foundation chain lines up behind the last row, stitch for stitch. Working through both layers, sl st in each st across. [20 sl st] Fasten off, leaving an 18 in (45 cm) tail, and turn the tube so the seam ridge is inside. The seam runs the same way as the ribs, so it blends in.

### Gather and stuff

1. Thread one long tail onto your tapestry needle. There should be one tail at each open end.
2. Weave a running stitch around that opening, in and out through the end of every row, about ¼ in (6 mm) from the edge.
3. Pull firmly to cinch it closed, take a couple of small stitches across the center to lock it, knot securely, and push the tail inside.
4. Stuff through the other opening until the pumpkin is plump but still soft. Overstuffing stretches the stitches and lets the filling show.
5. Gather the second opening the same way with the other tail, topping up the stuffing before you pull it fully closed.

### Shape the segments

Cut about 2½ yd (2.3 m) of pumpkin-colored yarn and thread your needle. Marking six evenly spaced points around the middle with pins first helps keep the segments even.

1. Insert the needle at the center top and push it straight down through the middle and out at the center bottom, leaving a 6 in (15 cm) tail at the top.
2. Carry the yarn up the outside to the center top, insert the needle there, and bring it out at the bottom again. Pull until the yarn sinks in and the top and bottom dimple slightly. That's one segment line.
3. Turn the pumpkin and repeat step 2 until you have 6 evenly spaced lines, like a pie cut into 6 slices.
4. Take the needle from the center bottom straight up through the middle to the top without wrapping the outside. Knot the working yarn and starting tail together, bury both ends inside, and trim. The stem will cover the knot.

### Attach the stem and leaf

Roll the stem tightly into a small cylinder, starting at the foundation-chain edge. Use the long tail to stitch along the outer edge so the roll stays closed, ending at one end of the stem, then sew that end to the center top. Sew the leaf and tendril beside the stem with their tails and weave in any remaining ends.

## Making it bigger or smaller

- **Foundation chain controls height.** Chain more for a taller pumpkin, fewer for a shorter one. Always chain 2 more than the number of hdc you want per row.
- **Rows control the width around.** For a round pumpkin, work until the rectangle is about twice as long as it is wide. Stop sooner for a taller shape, or keep going for a squat one, keeping the row count odd.
- **Change the yarn.** The same counts in bulky (#5) yarn with a 6.5 mm (US K-10½) hook make a bigger pumpkin; DK (#3) yarn with a 4 mm (US G-6) hook makes a smaller one. A hook slightly smaller than the label suggests keeps the fabric firm for stuffing.

These starting points assume worsted-weight yarn and a 5 mm (US H-8) hook. All rows and sizes are approximate.

| Size | Chain | Hdc per row | Rows | Approx. finished width |

| Small | 17 | 15 | 23 | about 3 in (7.5 cm) |
| Medium (as written) | 22 | 20 | 31 | about 4 in (10 cm) |
| Large | 32 | 30 | 45 | about 6 in (15 cm) |

## Tips

- **Mark the first stitch.** A stitch marker in the first hdc of each row shows you exactly where the last stitch is on the way back, which can be hard to spot in back-loop rows.
- **Stuff in small tufts.** Pulling fiberfill into small pieces before adding it gives a smoother shape.
- **Wrap snugly, not tightly.** Aim for gentle valleys rather than a pinched waist, and pull each wrap the same amount.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

- **A row with no ridge.** You probably worked through both loops. Unravel back to that row and rework it in BLO.
- **The stitch count drifts.** Ending up with 19 or 21 hdc usually means you skipped the first stitch or worked into the turning chain. Rip back to the last row with 20 hdc; our guide to [fixing uneven crochet edges](/blog/fix-uneven-crochet-edges) explains how to spot edge stitches.
- **A hole at the top or bottom.** The running stitch may be too close to the edge or skipping row ends. Work a second pass through the row ends and pull again.
- **Lopsided segments.** The needle probably isn't passing through the true center. Pin your six points first and check each wrap from above.

## Variations

- **Different colors:** Cream, sage, dusty pink, rust, or speckled yarn make a pretty mixed display.
- **Stripes:** Change color every few rows. Once the rectangle becomes a tube, the stripes run from top to bottom like the markings on a squash.
- **Natural stem:** Use a short twig or cinnamon stick instead, secured with a few stitches or a dab of fabric glue.
- **Mini pumpkins:** DK yarn and a shorter chain make tiny pumpkins for garlands or gift toppers.

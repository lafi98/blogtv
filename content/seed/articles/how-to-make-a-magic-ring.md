A magic ring is a way to begin crochet worked in the round so that the center pulls completely closed, leaving no hole. In this guide you'll learn two versions of it step by step, how to work and close the first round, and how to keep the center from loosening later on.

It's written for beginners who can already make a chain and a single crochet (UK double crochet) and are ready to try working in circles. If those stitches still feel new, spend a little time with [the basic crochet stitches](/blog/basic-crochet-stitches) first. The ring is much easier once your hands know the stitch.

## What is a magic ring?

A magic ring, also called a magic circle or adjustable ring, is a loop of yarn that you work your first round of stitches into. Once the stitches are in place, you pull the yarn tail and the loop shrinks like a drawstring, gathering the stitches into a tight circle.

That closed center is the whole point. It's popular for:

- **Amigurumi** (small stuffed toys), where a hole at the start would let stuffing peek through
- **Hats worked from the top down**, so there's no gap at the crown
- **Coasters, flat circles and round motifs**, for a neat, solid middle

Some patterns abbreviate it as "MR"; others write it out in full.

## What you'll need

- **Smooth, light-colored yarn.** Medium-weight (worsted) yarn is easiest to see while learning; dark or fuzzy yarn hides the ring.
- **A hook to suit your yarn.** The label suggests a size; for many worsted-weight yarns it's around 5 mm (US H-8). Amigurumi patterns often use a smaller hook for a firmer fabric.
- **A stitch marker**, or a safety pin or scrap of contrasting yarn.
- **A tapestry needle**, a blunt needle with a large eye, for weaving in the tail.
- **Scissors.**

## How to make a single-wrap magic ring

These steps are written for right-handed crocheters, who work around the ring counterclockwise. If you're left-handed, mirror the hand positions and work clockwise.

1. **Leave a tail.** Measure out about 6 in (15 cm) of yarn from the end. This tail is what you'll pull to close the ring, so it needs to be long enough to grip and to weave in later.
2. **Form a loop.** Make a circle roughly the size of a large coin, crossing the working yarn (the strand attached to the ball) over the tail. The tail hangs down below the crossing point.
3. **Pinch the cross.** Hold the point where the two strands cross between the thumb and middle finger of your non-hook hand. Keeping this point steady is the fiddly part at first, so go slowly.
4. **Pull up a loop.** Insert your hook through the center of the ring from front to back. Yarn over (wrap the working yarn over the hook from back to front) and pull it through to the front of the ring. You now have one loop on the hook.
5. **Chain 1.** Yarn over and pull through the loop on your hook. This chain anchors the ring. In most patterns it does not count as a stitch.
6. **Make the first stitch.** Insert your hook into the center of the ring again, making sure it goes under both the ring and the tail lying alongside it. Yarn over and pull up a loop (two loops on the hook), then yarn over and pull through both loops. That's one single crochet.
7. **Keep going.** Repeat step 6 until you have the number of stitches your pattern asks for. Six is the most common first round. Nudge the stitches around the ring as you go so they don't bunch up.

## Working and closing the first round

### Count before you close

It's far easier to count while the ring is still open and the stitches are spread out. Each single crochet has a small V-shaped top sitting along the ring. Don't include the beginning chain 1 in your count.

### Pull the ring closed

Hold your stitches lightly and pull the tail. The loop will shrink and the stitches will draw together into a circle. Keep pulling firmly until the hole disappears. If it won't close, jump to the troubleshooting table below.

### Join the round or work in a spiral

What comes next depends on your pattern:

- **Joined rounds:** Work a slip stitch into the top of the first single crochet (not into the chain 1). The next round usually starts with a chain.
- **Continuous spiral:** Many amigurumi patterns don't join at all. Instead, place a stitch marker in the first stitch of the next round and move it up as you go, so you always know where each round begins.

A typical second round for a flat circle is two single crochet in each stitch, which takes you from 6 stitches to 12.

## The double-wrap magic ring

The double-wrap version wraps the yarn around twice, so there are two strands in the ring instead of one. It takes a few extra seconds to close, but once both strands are tightened the center holds more securely. That makes it useful for slippery yarns and for toys that will be handled a lot.

1. Leave a 6 in (15 cm) tail and wrap the working yarn twice around two fingers of your non-hook hand, so two strands lie side by side.
2. Insert your hook under both strands, yarn over with the working yarn, and pull up a loop. Chain 1.
3. Work your stitches into the ring, going under both strands and the tail each time. You can slide the wraps off your fingers after the first stitch or two.
4. To close, give the tail a gentle tug and watch which of the two ring strands moves. Pull that strand, away from the stitches, until the other strand has closed completely.
5. Now pull the tail firmly to close the strand you just pulled. Both strands are now snug against the stitches.

## Securing the tail so it doesn't loosen

A magic ring is held shut by its tail, so if the tail slips, the center can gradually reopen with use and washing. A few habits keep it closed:

- **Wait a round or two.** Leave the tail hanging until you've worked a couple more rounds, so there's more fabric to weave through.
- **Tighten once more.** Just before weaving, give the tail a final pull to make sure the center is fully closed.
- **Weave in more than one direction.** Thread the tail onto a tapestry needle and, on the wrong side, run it through the backs of several stitches. Then turn and weave back the other way, ideally splitting the plies of the yarn as you go so the tail grips.
- **Hide the end in toys.** For stuffed pieces, weave the tail in, trim it, and let the end sit inside, where the stuffing covers it.

## The chain-and-join alternatives

If the magic ring feels awkward at first, two chain-based starts do a similar job, and many patterns name one of them directly.

- **Chain 2 and work into the 2nd chain from the hook.** Chain 2, then work all of your first-round stitches into the 2nd chain from the hook (the first chain you made). This leaves a small hole and is easy to learn.
- **Chain 4 and join.** Chain 4, then slip stitch into the first chain to form a ring. Work your first-round stitches into the center of that ring, not into the chains themselves. Some patterns use 5 or 6 chains instead. This leaves a visible hole, which is fine, and often intended, for many granny squares and lacy motifs.

Neither start can be pulled fully closed the way a magic ring can, which is why most amigurumi patterns favor the ring. If your pattern begins with a chain and you'd prefer a magic ring (or the other way around), you can usually swap one for the other, as long as you work the same number of stitches into it.

## Tips

- **Practice a handful of rings** on scrap yarn before you start a project. It's a motion your hands learn through repetition.
- **Keep the ring roomy while you work.** A generous loop is easier to get your hook into, and you'll be closing it anyway.
- **Use a stitch marker from the start**, especially in spiral rounds, where the beginning of each round is hard to see.
- **Choose the double wrap for slippery yarn.** Some cottons and silky blends tend to work loose, so the second strand is worth the extra moment.
- **Expect a tiny gap with bulky yarn.** Thick strands take up room in the center, so the ring may not close quite as completely as it does with thinner yarn.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

| Problem | Likely cause | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| The ring unravels when you pull the tail | No anchoring chain 1, or the strands weren't crossed | Start again, crossing the strands and chaining 1 before the first stitch |
| You pull and nothing closes | You're pulling the working yarn, not the tail | Pull the short end; the working yarn only tightens the loop on your hook |
| The ring closes only partway | A stitch went around the tail alone, or the hook split the tail | Pull out the round and rework it, inserting the hook into the center each time |
| A double-wrap ring won't close | The tail was pulled before the other strand | Find the strand that moves, pull it until the other closes, then pull the tail |
| Round 1 has the wrong count | The chain 1 was counted, or a stitch is hiding | Count before closing; the chain 1 usually doesn't count |
| The center reopens later | The tail was woven loosely or in one direction only | Tighten and reweave back and forth, or run matching yarn through the inner loops of round 1, pull snug and weave in |

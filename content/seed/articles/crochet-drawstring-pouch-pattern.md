This little drawstring pouch is a satisfying first bag. A flat circle of single crochet (UK double crochet) forms the base, one round worked in the back loops turns the edge upward, and straight rounds build the sides. An eyelet round near the top holds a cord, so the pouch cinches closed around coins, earbuds, stitch markers or a small gift.

If you can work single crochet and want to practice working in the round, this is a comfortable next step. Every round lists its stitch count, and the variations show you how to resize the pouch.

## Pattern at a glance

- **Skill level:** Easy
- **Approximate finished size:** base about 3¾ in (9.5 cm) across; sides about 4½ in (11.5 cm) tall; about 6 in (15 cm) wide when pressed flat. Your pouch may come out a little larger or smaller depending on your yarn and tension.
- **Yarn weight:** Medium (#4) worsted-weight cotton
- **Hook:** 4.5 mm (US 7)
- **Gauge (UK tension) note:** Exact gauge isn't critical for a pouch. The sizes above assume roughly 16 sc and 18 rounds to 4 in (10 cm). What matters most is a firm fabric without large gaps between stitches.

## What you'll need

- About 60 yd (55 m) of medium-weight cotton yarn, including the drawstring (approximate, with a little to spare)
- 4.5 mm (US 7) crochet hook
- 1 stitch marker, or a short piece of contrasting yarn
- Yarn needle
- Scissors
- Optional: 2 beads with holes large enough for your cord

Not sure whether a yarn counts as medium weight? The [Craft Yarn Council's yarn weight system](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards/yarn-weight-system) explains the numbered categories you'll see on yarn labels.

## Abbreviations (US terms)

- **ch:** chain
- **sc:** single crochet (UK double crochet)
- **sl st:** slip stitch
- **st(s):** stitch(es)
- **sk:** skip (UK miss)
- **sp(s):** space(s)
- **BLO:** back loop only
- **( ) × 6:** work the instructions inside the parentheses the number of times shown

This pattern uses US terms throughout. If you learned from UK patterns, the guide to [US vs UK crochet terms](/blog/us-vs-uk-crochet-terms) lays out every conversion side by side.

## Pattern notes

- The whole pouch is worked in **continuous spiral rounds**. Don't join or chain at the start of a round; just keep crocheting into the next stitch.
- Place a stitch marker in the first stitch of each round and move it up as you go. In a spiral, it's the only reliable way to see where a round begins.
- The number in parentheses is the total stitch count at the end of the round.
- Work through both loops of each stitch, except in Round 9.
- In the eyelet round, each chain-1 space counts as one stitch because it replaces the stitch you skip, so the count stays at 48. In the next round, you work one sc into each chain-1 space.
- The standard increase repeat can give the base a faintly six-sided outline. For a rounder base, the [round coaster pattern](/blog/round-crochet-coasters-pattern) shows how to stagger the increases; its counts for Rounds 1–8 match these exactly.

## The pattern

### Base

Start with a magic ring; the guide on [how to make a magic ring](/blog/how-to-make-a-magic-ring) walks you through it. Alternatively, chain 2 and work Round 1 into the second chain from the hook.

**Round 1:** 6 sc into the magic ring. Pull the tail to close the ring and place a marker in the first stitch. (6)

**Round 2:** 2 sc in each st around. (12)

**Round 3:** (Sc in next st, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (18)

**Round 4:** (Sc in next 2 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (24)

**Round 5:** (Sc in next 3 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (30)

**Round 6:** (Sc in next 4 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (36)

**Round 7:** (Sc in next 5 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (42)

**Round 8:** (Sc in next 6 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6. (48)

The base should lie flat and measure roughly 3¾ in (9.5 cm) across. If it waves or cups, count before going further.

### Sides

**Round 9 (turning round):** Working in back loops only, sc in each st around. (48)

Working into just the back loop leaves the front loops as a ridge on the outside, and the fabric bends upward at a neat corner. The back loop is the one farther from you as you look down at the stitch.

![Diagram of the top of a crochet stitch seen from above, showing its V shape with the front loop nearest the viewer and the back loop farther away, each labeled](/images/diagrams/front-and-back-loops.svg)

**Rounds 10–24:** Sc in each st around, through both loops. (48)

That's 15 plain rounds. The sides should rise straight up; if they flare or narrow, a stitch has probably been added or skipped near the marker.

### Eyelet round

**Round 25:** (Sc in next 2 sts, ch 1, sk next st) × 16. (48: 32 sc and 16 ch-1 sps)

Here's why the count doesn't change. Each repeat uses three stitches from the round below (two worked, one skipped) and creates three stitches (two sc plus one ch-1 space). Sixteen repeats of three stitches make 48, matching the round below. The 16 small holes are your eyelets.

### Top band

**Round 26:** Sc in each sc around, working 1 sc into each ch-1 sp. Insert the hook into the space under the chain rather than into the chain itself. (48)

**Rounds 27–28:** Sc in each st around. (48)

After the last stitch of Round 28, sl st into the next st. Fasten off, leaving a 6 in (15 cm) tail.

## Finishing

### Weave in the ends

The slip stitch at the end of Round 28 softens the small step every spiral leaves where it ends. Weave both tails through the backs of several stitches, changing direction once or twice; smooth cotton tails can work loose otherwise.

### Make the drawstring

With the same hook, chain until the cord measures about 24 in (61 cm), roughly twice the pouch's circumference. That length lets the pouch open fully with enough left over to tie.

A plain chain works, but it can be thin and stretchy. For a firmer, rounder cord, don't fasten off after chaining. Instead, sl st into the back bump of the second chain from the hook and into the back bump of each chain along the cord, then fasten off.

### Thread the drawstring

Thread one end of the cord onto a yarn needle or a small safety pin. Starting on the outside, go in through one eyelet, out through the next, and keep weaving in and out all the way around. Because there are 16 eyelets, an even number, both ends finish on the outside, side by side.

### Knot or bead the ends

Tie an overhand knot near each end, or knot both ends together, so the cord can't slip back through the eyelets. For a decorative touch, slide a bead onto each end before knotting and trim the tails into small tassels. Leave beads off pouches meant for babies or young children, since small parts can come loose.

## Tips

- Count at the marker every few rounds, and always after Rounds 8, 9 and 25.
- Spiral rounds of single crochet lean slightly, so the stitch columns look gently twisted. That's normal.
- If small items might slip through, try a 4 mm (US G-6) hook for denser fabric. The pouch will come out a little smaller.
- For stripes, change color on the final yarn over of the last stitch before the marker. Expect a small step at each change; spirals always have one.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

- **The base curls up like a bowl.** You've probably missed an increase, or your tension tightened. Count; if it's short, unravel to the error. If the count is right, try a slightly larger hook for the base.
- **The base ripples.** There's likely an extra increase. Unravel to the round where the count went wrong.
- **There's no crisp corner between base and sides.** Round 9 was probably worked through both loops. Unravel it and rework it in back loops only.
- **The eyelet round has the wrong count.** Forgetting to skip a stitch adds stitches; forgetting the chain loses them. You should have 32 sc and 16 spaces, 48 in total.
- **The top flares above the eyelets.** Make sure you worked exactly one sc into each ch-1 space in Round 26, not one into the chain and another into the space.
- **The pouch won't open fully.** The cord is too short. Make a new one about twice the pouch's circumference.

## Variations

### Resize the pouch

The base works at any multiple of 6. Stop increasing early for a smaller pouch, or keep going for a larger one, adding one plain stitch to each repeat per round: (sc in next 7 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6 gives 54, then (sc in next 8 sts, 2 sc in next st) × 6 gives 60. Work the back-loop turning round and everything after it over your new count, renumbering the rounds.

- Stopping at Round 6 (36 sts) gives a pouch about 4½ in (11.5 cm) wide when flat, with 12 eyelets.
- Increasing to 60 sts gives a pouch about 7½ in (19 cm) wide when flat, with 20 eyelets.

The eyelet repeat always fits: any multiple of 6 divides evenly by 3 and gives an even number of eyelets, so both cord ends still finish on the outside.

To change the height, add or remove plain rounds before the eyelet round. At this gauge, each round adds a little under ¼ in (about 5–6 mm).

### Two drawstrings

For a pouch that closes from both sides, make two cords. Thread the first as above. Start the second on the opposite side, between the 8th and 9th eyelets counting from where the first cord's ends exit, and weave it all the way around. Pulling both pairs closes the pouch evenly.

### Closer eyelets

For a tighter gather, work Round 25 as (sc in next st, ch 1, sk next st) × 24. The count is still 48 (24 sc and 24 ch-1 sps), giving you 24 eyelets.

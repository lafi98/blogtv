Not every handmade gift needs weeks of work. Some of the most useful crochet gifts are small enough to finish in an evening or two, and they're a great way to practice basic stitches while making something people will actually use.

Below are eleven ideas for beginners and hobbyists, each with suggested yarn, a rough difficulty level and a time range. You'll also find tips on presenting your gift and writing a care label so it lasts.

## A quick note on time estimates

The time ranges below are rough guides only. They assume you're comfortable with stitches like single crochet (UK double crochet) and double crochet (UK treble). Everyone works at a different pace, so you may be faster or slower, and that's completely normal. If you're newer to crochet, expect the upper end of each range or a little beyond.

For an estimate based on your own speed, time yourself crocheting a small swatch, as explained in our [holiday gift planning timeline](/blog/holiday-crochet-planning-timeline).

Difficulty uses common skill labels: Beginner, Easy and Intermediate.

## What you'll need

Most of these projects use:

- A hook to suit your yarn, often 4–6 mm (US G-6 to J-10) for DK to worsted weight
- Small amounts of yarn, often less than one skein
- A yarn needle for weaving in ends
- Scissors and a few stitch markers
- Optional extras: plain elastic hair ties, metal key rings, buttons, ribbon

## Eleven quick gift ideas

### 1. Dishcloths

**Yarn:** 100% cotton, worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Beginner · **Rough time:** 1–3 hours

Dishcloths are practical, washable and used every day. Cotton absorbs water and stands up to frequent washing, which makes it a better choice here than acrylic. A plain square of single crochet looks neat, and a textured stitch adds scrubbing power. Two or three tied with twine make a tidy gift.

### 2. Round coasters

**Yarn:** Cotton, DK (#3) or worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Beginner · **Rough time:** about 20–60 minutes each

Coasters are quick, use very little yarn and naturally come as a set. Cotton copes well with warm mugs and damp glasses. Our [Round Crochet Coasters Pattern](/blog/round-crochet-coasters-pattern) walks you through working flat circles in the round.

### 3. Granny-square coasters

**Yarn:** Cotton or cotton blend, DK (#3) or worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Beginner to Easy · **Rough time:** about 20–45 minutes each, plus time for ends

A small granny square makes a cheerful square coaster and a good way to use up scraps. Follow the [Classic Granny Square Pattern](/blog/classic-granny-square-pattern) and stop when your square measures about 4 inches (10 cm) across; the number of rounds depends on your yarn and hook. Each color change adds ends to weave in, so allow extra finishing time.

### 4. Headbands and ear warmers

**Yarn:** Soft wool, wool blend or acrylic, worsted (#4) or bulky (#5) · **Difficulty:** Beginner to Easy · **Rough time:** 1–3 hours

An ear warmer is cozy, practical and forgiving on fit, especially in a stretchy ribbed stitch. Many are simply a rectangle seamed into a loop, sometimes with a twist at the front. Measure around the head, or use the adult size given in your pattern, and make the band slightly smaller so it stays in place.

### 5. Bookmarks

**Yarn:** Crochet thread or fine cotton, lace (#0) to light (#3) · **Difficulty:** Easy (thread can feel fiddly at first) · **Rough time:** 30–90 minutes

Bookmarks suit readers of any age and slip easily into a card. Thinner yarn keeps the bookmark flat enough for the book to close properly. A long, narrow strip with a tassel is simple; a small motif on a chain tail is a fun next step. Thread usually calls for a small steel hook, so check the label.

### 6. Scrunchies

**Yarn:** Soft cotton, cotton blend or acrylic, DK (#3) or worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Beginner · **Rough time:** about 20–45 minutes each

A scrunchie starts with single crochet worked all the way around a plain elastic hair tie, followed by a round or two of taller stitches for a ruffled look. They're fast, colorful and useful. Smooth, soft yarn tends to feel more comfortable in hair than fuzzy or scratchy fibers.

### 7. Small drawstring pouches

**Yarn:** Cotton or cotton blend, DK (#3) or worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Easy · **Rough time:** 1.5–4 hours

Pouches hold jewelry, earbuds or small treasures, so they double as gift wrap for a second small present. Cotton gives them good structure. The [Crochet Drawstring Pouch Pattern](/blog/crochet-drawstring-pouch-pattern) covers the base, body and drawstring.

### 8. Plant-pot covers

**Yarn:** Cotton or acrylic, worsted (#4) or bulky (#5) · **Difficulty:** Easy · **Rough time:** 1–3 hours, depending on pot size

A cover dresses up a plain pot. Work a flat circle to match the base, then work rounds upward, adding occasional increases if the pot flares outward. Measure the pot's base and height before you start. Use the cover over a pot with a saucer or a watertight inner pot so the yarn doesn't stay wet.

### 9. Mug cozies

**Yarn:** Cotton or wool blend, worsted (#4) · **Difficulty:** Easy · **Rough time:** about 45 minutes to 1.5 hours

A mug cozy is a strip that wraps around a mug and fastens with a button, loop or tie, leaving space for the handle. Measure around the mug and the height between the top and bottom of the handle. Gifting it on a mug also solves the wrapping question.

### 10. Simple hats in bulky yarn

**Yarn:** Wool, wool blend or acrylic, bulky (#5) or super bulky (#6), often with an 8–10 mm (US L-11 to N/P-15) hook · **Difficulty:** Easy to Intermediate · **Rough time:** 2–5 hours

Thick yarn and a large hook make a hat grow quickly. Hats need more attention to sizing than flat items, so check your gauge and try the hat on, or measure it, as you go. A pom-pom made from leftover yarn is an easy finishing touch.

### 11. Keychains and bag charms

**Yarn:** Scraps of cotton or acrylic, DK (#3) or worsted (#4), plus a metal key ring · **Difficulty:** Easy · **Rough time:** 30–90 minutes

Small hearts, stars or round shapes on a key ring make fun add-on gifts and use up leftovers. Because they have small, hard parts, keychains suit older children and adults rather than babies or toddlers.

## How to present a handmade gift

- **Finish carefully.** Weave in ends securely, trim them close, and lightly block flat items so the edges sit neatly. Let anything you've blocked dry completely before wrapping.
- **Bundle a set.** Tie coasters or dishcloths with ribbon or twine, or slip a mug cozy onto a mug.
- **Let the gift be the wrapping.** A pouch can hold a second small gift.
- **Keep packaging simple.** Tissue paper, a small box or a clean reused jar all work well.

## Add a care label

A care card helps your gift last, and it's thoughtful for anyone who prefers to avoid certain fibers. Copy the details from your yarn label onto a small card or tag:

- **Fiber content:** for example, "100% cotton" or "80% acrylic, 20% wool"
- **Washing:** for example, "Machine wash warm" or "Hand wash in cool water"
- **Drying:** for example, "Lay flat to dry" or "Reshape while damp"

Wool that isn't labeled superwash can felt, meaning it shrinks and thickens, if it's machine washed, so a clear "hand wash, lay flat to dry" note matters for those pieces. If you used more than one yarn, write down the gentlest of their care instructions.

## Tips

- Choose one or two patterns and make them in several colors to save time.
- Use cotton for anything that touches water or hot mugs.
- Make one sample first to check the size before committing to a whole set.
- Keep a box of finished small items through the year so you're never starting from zero.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

- **Using acrylic for dishcloths.** Acrylic doesn't absorb water as well as cotton. Fix: use 100% cotton for anything meant for wiping or drying.
- **Skipping measurements.** Mug cozies, pot covers and hats all depend on fit. Fix: measure the object or person before starting, and check as you go.
- **Making bulky bookmarks.** Thick yarn can strain a book's spine. Fix: switch to thread or a fine yarn with a smaller hook.
- **Rushing the ends.** Loose ends can work free in the wash. Fix: weave each end through the stitches in a couple of different directions before trimming.
- **Wrapping damp items.** Fix: give blocked pieces enough time to dry fully before they go in a box.

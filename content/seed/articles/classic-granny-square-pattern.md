The granny square is one of crochet's most recognizable motifs: little groups of three double crochet (UK treble crochet) stitches, separated by small chain spaces and worked outward from the center in rounds. One square makes a coaster-sized motif; a stack of them can become a blanket, a pillow cover, or a tote.

This pattern suits you if you're comfortable with chains, slip stitches, and double crochet and you're ready for your first project in the round. If double crochet still feels new, practice with our guide to [the basic crochet stitches](/blog/basic-crochet-stitches) first. Since every round can be a different color, it's also a lovely way to use up leftover yarn.

## Pattern at a glance

- **Skill level:** Beginner
- **Finished size (approximate):** about 4–5 in (10–13 cm) square after Round 4 in worsted-weight yarn, with each extra round adding very roughly 1 in (2.5 cm). Your size depends on your yarn and tension.
- **Yarn weight:** Medium / worsted (#4), though any weight works
- **Hook:** 5 mm (US H-8), or the size suggested on your yarn label
- **Gauge note:** Gauge isn't important for a single square. If you plan to join several, make them all with the same yarn and hook so they match.

## What you'll need

- Worsted-weight yarn in one or more colors: very roughly 10–20 yd (9–18 m) per 4-round square, depending on your tension, so scraps are ideal
- 5 mm (US H-8) crochet hook
- Tapestry needle (a blunt, large-eyed yarn needle)
- Scissors
- Optional: a stitch marker, plus rustproof pins and a towel or foam mat for blocking

## Abbreviations (US terms)

If you usually follow UK patterns, our [US vs UK crochet terms](/blog/us-vs-uk-crochet-terms) guide lines them up side by side. The Craft Yarn Council also lists [standard crochet abbreviations](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards/crochet-abbreviations).

- **ch:** chain
- **sl st:** slip stitch
- **dc:** double crochet (UK treble crochet)
- **sp(s):** space(s); a ch-1 sp or ch-2 sp is the space under a chain-1 or chain-2 from the previous round
- **rep:** repeat
- **cluster:** in this pattern, 3 dc worked into the same space
- **\*:** repeat the steps after the asterisk as directed
- **( ):** work everything inside the parentheses into the same space
- **[ ]:** the cluster and space counts at the end of a round

## Pattern notes

- Work in rounds with the right side facing you. Don't turn between rounds.
- The ch 3 at the start of each round counts as the first dc, and the bracketed counts include it. Close each round with a sl st into the top chain of that beginning ch-3.
- Every cluster goes into a space (the center ring, a corner ch-2 sp, or a side ch-1 sp), not into the tops of the stitches below.
- Each corner is (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in one ch-2 sp. Along the sides, clusters are separated by ch 1.
- A [magic ring](/blog/how-to-make-a-magic-ring) pulls the center fully closed. A chain ring is easier to hold but leaves a small hole.

## The pattern

**Start:** Make a magic ring, or ch 4 and join with a sl st to form a ring.

**Round 1:** Ch 3 (counts as dc), 2 dc in ring, ch 2. \*3 dc in ring, ch 2; rep from \* 2 more times. If you used a magic ring, pull the tail to close the center. Join with a sl st to the top of the beginning ch-3. [4 clusters (12 dc), 4 ch-2 sps]

**Round 2:** Sl st in next 2 dc and in next ch-2 sp, so your hook is in a corner. Ch 3 (counts as dc), (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in same sp. \*Ch 1, (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 sp; rep from \* 2 more times. Ch 1, join with a sl st to the top of the beginning ch-3. [8 clusters (24 dc), 4 ch-2 corner sps, 4 ch-1 sps]

**Round 3:** Sl st in next 2 dc and in next ch-2 corner sp. Ch 3, (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in same sp. \*Ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 corner sp; rep from \* 2 more times. Ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, join with a sl st to the top of the beginning ch-3. [12 clusters (36 dc), 4 ch-2 corner sps, 8 ch-1 sps]

**Round 4:** Sl st in next 2 dc and in next ch-2 corner sp. Ch 3, (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in same sp. \*Ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in next ch-2 corner sp; rep from \* 2 more times. Ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp, ch 1, join with a sl st to the top of the beginning ch-3. [16 clusters (48 dc), 4 ch-2 corner sps, 12 ch-1 sps]

Fasten off and weave in your ends, or keep going.

### The rule for any round

Every round after Round 1 works the same way: (3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in each corner sp, 3 dc in each ch-1 sp along the sides, and ch 1 between groups. Each side gains one ch-1 sp per round, so it also gains one cluster.

Counting the corner clusters at each end of a side, **Round N has N clusters per side, 4 × N clusters and 12 × N dc in total, 4 × (N − 1) ch-1 sps, and always 4 corner sps.** To work any round from Round 2 on, start in a corner as usual, then on each side repeat "ch 1, 3 dc in next ch-1 sp" N − 2 times before the final ch 1 and the next corner.

| Round | Clusters per side | Total clusters | Total dc | Ch-1 sps | Ch-2 corners |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 4 | 12 | 0 | 4 |
| 2 | 2 | 8 | 24 | 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 12 | 36 | 8 | 4 |
| 4 | 4 | 16 | 48 | 12 | 4 |
| 5 | 5 | 20 | 60 | 16 | 4 |
| 6 | 6 | 24 | 72 | 20 | 4 |

In Round 1, each side is a single cluster sitting between two corner spaces.

## Changing colors

Switching colors at the start of a round gives the classic patchwork look.

1. Finish the round with the usual join. Cut the yarn, leaving a 6 in (15 cm) tail, and pull it through the loop on your hook.
2. Place a slip knot of the new color on your hook, insert the hook into any ch-2 corner sp, and sl st to join. Ch 3 (counts as dc).
3. Work (2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc) in that same corner sp, then continue the round as written.

Since you join straight into a corner, skip the slip stitches that normally move you there. Working the next few clusters over the old and new tails saves weaving later.

## Finishing

### Weaving in ends

Weave each tail through the backs of the stitches with your tapestry needle, changing direction at least once so it stays put. Where you can, hide tails in stitches of the same color. If you used a magic ring, weave the starting tail around the center first to keep the hole closed.

### Blocking

Blocking evens out the stitches and squares off the corners. Pin the square to size on a towel or foam mat, mist it until damp, and let it dry completely. For acrylic, hover a steam iron just above the pinned square without touching it, since direct heat can permanently flatten the yarn. Check the care symbols on [your yarn label](/blog/how-to-read-a-yarn-label) before choosing a method.

### Joining squares

Each side of a 4-round square has 12 dc and 3 ch-1 sps between its corner spaces, so squares made the same way line up stitch for stitch.

- **Whipstitch:** Hold two squares right sides together. With a tapestry needle, sew over the edge through each matching pair of stitches from corner to corner, then open the squares out flat.
- **Slip-stitch join:** Sl st through both layers along the edge. With wrong sides together, the join makes a decorative ridge on the front; with right sides together, the ridge sits on the back. Keep your tension relaxed so the seam doesn't pucker.

## Tips

- **Mark the join.** A stitch marker in the top of the beginning ch-3 makes the join easy to find.
- **Mind the last ch-1 space.** The final ch-1 sp before the join is the easiest one to miss in the next round.
- **Watch the shape.** If the square cups like a bowl, your chains may be too tight; relax them or try ch 3 corners. If the edges ripple, your chains may be too loose.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

- **Joining into the wrong stitch.** Joining into the first dc instead of the top of the ch-3 throws off the next round. Undo the join and use the top chain of the beginning ch-3.
- **A side with an extra or missing cluster.** A ch-1 sp was probably skipped or worked twice. Count each side against the table and rip back to where the counts first go wrong.
- **A rounded corner.** The ch 2 between corner clusters is missing. Check for exactly 4 ch-2 sps at the end of every round.
- **Working into stitches instead of spaces.** If the square looks solid and ripples, insert the hook under the chains, not into the tops of the dc.

## Variations

- **Sharper corners:** Make every corner (3 dc, ch 3, 3 dc). The cluster counts stay the same; only the corner spaces grow by one chain.
- **One big granny:** Follow the rule for Round N until the square is blanket-sized.
- **Single crochet border:** Finish with a round of single crochet (UK double crochet), abbreviated sc: 1 sc in each dc and each ch-1 sp, and 3 sc in each corner sp. On a 4-round square that's 15 sc per side plus 3 in each corner, for 72 sc in total.
- **Cotton coasters:** In cotton yarn, one square makes a sturdy coaster. For a round version, try our [round crochet coasters pattern](/blog/round-crochet-coasters-pattern).

## Keep going, one round at a time

Your first granny square may not be perfectly even, and that's normal. The rhythm of cluster, chain, cluster tends to settle in after a few squares, and blocking smooths out many small differences.

Coasters are one of the best small projects for learning to crochet in the round. Each is a flat circle of single crochet (UK double crochet) that grows by six stitches a round, and once you understand why it stays flat, you can make circles of any size. You'll also learn how to shift your increases so the coaster stays round rather than six-sided, and how to add a contrasting edge.

It suits beginners who know single crochet, and anyone who wants a quick, practical set to keep or give.

## Pattern at a glance

- **Skill level:** Beginner
- **Approximate finished size:** about 4 in (10 cm) across with the edging, or about 3¾ in (9.5 cm) without it. Expect some variation with different yarns and tension.
- **Yarn weight:** Medium (#4) worsted-weight cotton
- **Hook:** 4 mm (US G-6)
- **Gauge (UK tension) note:** Gauge isn't critical, but dense fabric makes a better coaster. The size above assumes roughly 18 sc and 20 rounds to 4 in (10 cm). As a quick check, the circle should measure about 1½–1¾ in (4–4.5 cm) across after Round 4.

## What you'll need

- Main color: about 12 yd (11 m) of medium-weight cotton per coaster
- Contrast color (optional, for the edging): about 3 yd (3 m) per coaster
- 4 mm (US G-6) crochet hook, plus a 4.5 mm (US 7) hook if your slip stitches tend to be tight
- 1 stitch marker
- Yarn needle and scissors
- For blocking: a folded towel or blocking mat, rust-proof pins and a spray bottle

Amounts are approximate and include extra for tails. A set of four uses roughly 50 yd (46 m) of the main color.

## Why cotton suits coasters

Cotton absorbs moisture, so condensation from a cold glass soaks in instead of pooling on the table. It copes well with warmth, since it doesn't melt under a hot mug, and it lies flat and generally washes well.

Acrylic works, but it absorbs very little water, so drips tend to bead up and run off, and as a synthetic it can soften or lose shape under high heat. Either way, a single layer of crochet isn't a trivet, so keep hot pans on a proper one.

## Abbreviations (US terms)

- **ch:** chain
- **sc:** single crochet (UK double crochet)
- **inc:** increase; work 2 sc in the same stitch
- **sl st:** slip stitch
- **rsc:** reverse single crochet, also called crab stitch (UK reverse double crochet)
- **st(s):** stitch(es)
- **( ) × 6:** repeat the instructions in parentheses the number of times shown

More standard terms are listed in the Craft Yarn Council's [crochet abbreviations](https://www.craftyarncouncil.com/standards/crochet-abbreviations), and the guide to [US vs UK crochet terms](/blog/us-vs-uk-crochet-terms) converts each stitch name.

## Pattern notes

- This coaster is worked in **joined rounds**. Each round starts with ch 1, which doesn't count as a stitch, and ends with a sl st into the first sc; "join" means this slip stitch.
- The first sc of every round goes into the same stitch as the join, at the base of the ch 1. Mark it so it's easy to find next round.
- Don't work into the joining sl st. It looks like a stitch, but crocheting into it adds an extra one.
- The number in parentheses is the stitch count at the end of the round, not counting the ch 1 or the join.

### Why stagger the increases?

If every round uses the same kind of repeat, such as (sc in next 3 sts, inc) × 6, each new increase lands on top of the one below. Six lines of increases build up, each forming a slight corner, and by about Round 6 the circle starts to look like a hexagon.

Staggering moves the increases on alternate rounds. Rounds 4, 6 and 8 begin with about half the plain stitches, place an increase, work the usual repeat, and finish with the remaining plain stitches. The stitch count is identical; only the increase positions change, so the edge stays round.

## The pattern

Make a magic ring with the main color; the guide to [making a magic ring](/blog/how-to-make-a-magic-ring) covers it step by step, and [basic crochet stitches](/blog/basic-crochet-stitches) offers a single crochet refresher.

**Round 1:** Ch 1, 6 sc into the ring. Pull the tail to close the ring, then join. (6)

**Round 2:** Ch 1, inc in each st around; join. (12)

**Round 3:** Ch 1, (sc in next st, inc in next st) × 6; join. (18)

**Round 4 (staggered):** Ch 1, sc in next st, inc in next st, (sc in next 2 sts, inc in next st) × 5, sc in next st; join. (24)

**Round 5:** Ch 1, (sc in next 3 sts, inc in next st) × 6; join. (30)

**Round 6 (staggered):** Ch 1, sc in next 2 sts, inc in next st, (sc in next 4 sts, inc in next st) × 5, sc in next 2 sts; join. (36)

**Round 7:** Ch 1, (sc in next 5 sts, inc in next st) × 6; join. (42)

**Round 8 (staggered):** Ch 1, sc in next 3 sts, inc in next st, (sc in next 6 sts, inc in next st) × 5, sc in next 3 sts; join. (48)

**Round 9:** Ch 1, (sc in next 7 sts, inc in next st) × 6; join. (54)

For a contrasting edge, fasten off the main color now. To skip the edging, see Finishing.

### Round-by-round stitch counts

Here, "sc 3" means sc in each of the next 3 stitches.

| Round | What you do | Stitch count |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 sc in magic ring | 6 |
| 2 | inc in each st | 12 |
| 3 | (sc 1, inc) × 6 | 18 |
| 4 | sc 1, inc, (sc 2, inc) × 5, sc 1 | 24 |
| 5 | (sc 3, inc) × 6 | 30 |
| 6 | sc 2, inc, (sc 4, inc) × 5, sc 2 | 36 |
| 7 | (sc 5, inc) × 6 | 42 |
| 8 | sc 3, inc, (sc 6, inc) × 5, sc 3 | 48 |
| 9 | (sc 7, inc) × 6 | 54 |
| 10 (optional) | sl st or rsc in each st | 54 |

### Round 10: contrasting edge (optional)

Both options are worked into all 54 stitches, so the count stays the same.

**Slip stitch edge:** Make a slip knot with the contrast color and place it on your hook. Sl st into any st, then sl st into each remaining st around. (54) Keep them loose; tight slip stitches pull the edge in and make the coaster cup, so switch to a 4.5 mm (US 7) hook if needed.

**Crab stitch edge:** Join the contrast color with a sl st in any st and ch 1. Work backward, from left to right (right to left if you're left-handed): insert the hook into the next stitch to the right, yarn over, pull up a loop, then yarn over and pull through both loops. Rsc in each st around, ending in the stitch where you joined. (54) Join to the first rsc. The result is a raised, corded border that wears well.

## Finishing

### Fasten off neatly

For the smoothest edge, replace the final joining slip stitch with an invisible join. Cut the yarn and pull the tail out through the last stitch. Thread it onto a needle, pass it under both top loops of the first stitch of the round, then go back down through the top of the last stitch and snug it to match its neighbors.

Without the edging, finish Round 9 this way for a coaster about 3¾ in (9.5 cm) across.

### Weave in the ends

Weave each tail through the backs of the stitches, changing direction a couple of times. Pull the magic ring closed once more with the starting tail, then weave it around the center so the hole stays shut.

### Block flat

Blocking settles the stitches and relaxes a slight curl. Mist or briefly soak the coaster and press out the excess water without wringing. Smooth it into a circle on a towel or blocking mat, pin the edge if needed, and let it dry completely. Cotton can also be steamed with an iron held just above the surface. Blocking evens out small quirks but won't fix a stitch-count problem.

## Making a matching set

- Use the same yarn and hook throughout, and make the set within a few days if you can, since tension drifts.
- After Round 4 of each coaster, compare it with the first and adjust your hook if it's noticeably different.
- Trace a 4 in (10 cm) circle onto paper, cover it with clear plastic on your blocking surface, and pin every coaster to that outline.
- Swap main and contrast colors on alternate coasters. A stack of four tied with cord makes an easy present; find more in these [quick crochet gift ideas](/blog/quick-crochet-gift-ideas).

## Tips

- Count every round after Round 5, when increases are farther apart and easier to miss.
- Watch your tension as rounds get longer; it's easy to tighten up without noticing.
- Pull the magic ring closed before joining Round 1. It's much harder to tighten later.

## Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

- **Cupping (the edge curls up into a bowl):** Too few increases, usually a missed one, or tightening tension. Count; if the round is short, unravel to the mistake. If the count is right, try a hook half a size larger. A very mild cup often blocks out.
- **Ruffling (the edge goes wavy):** Too many increases, often from working into the joining slip stitch. Count and correct. If the count is right, your stitches may be loose; try a hook half a size smaller.
- **A hexagon shape:** The same repeat was used every round. Follow the staggered rounds as written.
- **The edging pulls the coaster into a bowl:** The slip stitches are too tight. Rework them with a larger hook.
- **A hole in the center:** The magic ring wasn't pulled tight. Tug the starting tail and weave it around the center.

## Variations

- **Larger coaster:** Before the edging, add a staggered Round 10: ch 1, sc in next 4 sts, inc in next st, (sc in next 8 sts, inc in next st) × 5, sc in next 4 sts; join. (60) Work the edging over 60 stitches. To keep going, alternate regular and staggered rounds, adding 6 stitches each time.
- **Double-thick coaster:** Make two circles through Round 9, hold them wrong sides together with stitches aligned, and work the edging through both loops of both layers to join them.
